Phishing Knowledge Base

1. Understanding Phishing

Phishing is a type of social engineering attack where malicious actors impersonate legitimate organizations or contacts to deceive individuals into revealing sensitive information.

Why attackers use it: It relies on human psychology rather than breaking complex software security. Deceiving a user into handing over credentials is often easier than hacking a system directly.

Common phishing methods:

  • Email Phishing: Fraudulent mass emails demanding immediate action.
  • Spear Phishing: Targeted messages tailored to a specific person or workplace.
  • Smishing & Vishing: Phishing attempted via SMS texts or telephone voice calls.

2. Warning Signs

Always inspect incoming communications for these frequent red flags:

  • Urgency: Demands for immediate action (e.g., "Account suspended in 30 minutes").
  • Suspicious Links: Hovering over links reveals mismatched web addresses.
  • Unexpected Attachments: Unsolicited PDFs, ZIPs, or executable files.
  • Requests for Sensitive Information: Asking for passwords, PINs, or card numbers.
  • Unknown Senders: Unfamiliar email addresses or slight variations of official domain names.

3. Suspicious URLs

Attackers construct deceptive URLs to trick victims into believing they are visiting legitimate websites.

Misspelled domains (Typosquatting):

Deceptive: paypaI-security-verify.com (uses capital 'i' or altered spelling)
Legitimate: paypal.com

Deceptive Subdomains:

Deceptive: update-bank-login.net/login

Notice that the actual domain name is "update-bank-login.net", not your bank's actual official domain name.

HTTPS Limitations:

A padlock icon or "https://" simply means the connection is encrypted. It does not guarantee that the website owner is trustworthy. Malicious sites routinely obtain valid HTTPS certificates.

4. Social Engineering

Social engineering manipulates human emotion to bypass logical decision-making. Common psychological triggers include:

  • Authority: Pretending to be an executive, IT administrator, or law enforcement official.
  • Fear: Threatening legal consequences, fine penalties, or account termination.
  • Urgency: Imposing artificially tight deadlines to prevent careful review.
  • Curiosity: Promising intriguing or exclusive information.
  • Rewards: Offering fake lottery wins, gift cards, or bonus payouts.
